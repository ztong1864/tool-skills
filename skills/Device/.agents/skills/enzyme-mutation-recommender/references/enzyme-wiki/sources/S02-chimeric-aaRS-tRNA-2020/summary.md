# S02 - Chimeric aaRS/tRNA Design, 2020

PDF: [paper.pdf](paper.pdf)

Original title: "Chimeric design of pyrrolysyl-tRNA synthetase/tRNA pairs and canonical synthetase/tRNA pairs for genetic code expansion"

## Scope

This paper uses the PylRS tRNA-binding domain and PylT structural features as transferable orthogonality modules to build chimeric aaRS/tRNA systems. It is primarily a system-design paper, but it is important for interpreting the role of the PylRS tRNA-binding domain.

## Scaffold And Numbering

- Chimeric synthetases fuse PylRS tRNA-binding domain elements with catalytic domains from canonical synthetases such as HisRS and PheRS.
- Several constructs use PylRS TD mutations previously associated with improved amber suppression, including IPYE-like variants.
- Numbering is construct-specific and should not be mapped to full-length MmPylRS or MbPylRS unless the exact domain source and boundary are known.

## Assays And Substrates

- GFP reporter assays in E. coli and mammalian cells.
- chHisRS systems suppress His at amber positions.
- chPheRS systems incorporate Phe, Tyr, Trp analogs, including fluorescent Trp analogs and modified aromatic amino acids.

## Mutation Evidence

- Chimeric HisRS constructs carrying PylRS TD variants with IPYE mutations showed higher amber suppression than corresponding constructs without the evolved TD state.
- PylRS TD placement and fusion orientation affected activity, indicating that domain architecture is part of the evidence, not just mutation identity.

## Mechanistic Evidence

- Direct evidence: PylRS/pylT orthogonality relies on unusual tRNA structural features and a PylRS TD that recognizes the tRNA without using the anticodon as a primary identity element.
- Cross-paper synthesis: improving a tRNA-binding domain can enhance amber suppression even when the catalytic domain is not PylRS, supporting the separability of tRNA recruitment and substrate chemistry.

## Caveats

- The paper does not directly recommend point mutations in full-length MmPylRS for D-amino-acid substrates.
- Chimeric construct behavior may depend on fusion boundaries, promoter context, tRNA design, and host cell.
