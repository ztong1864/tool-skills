# Substrate Range And Active-Site Coupling

## Direct Evidence

- [S01](../sources/S01-polysubstrate-pylrs-2023/summary.md) shows that homologous active-site variants MbPylRS(N311A/C313A) and MmPylRS(N346A/C348A) differ in incorporation efficiency across Phe analogs.
- [S06](../sources/S06-linker-ntd-engineering-2020/summary.md) shows that R61K/H63Y/S193R, remote from the primary substrate pocket, can shift substrate range in wild-type MmPylRS.
- [S07](../sources/S07-ml-guided-pylrs-2025/summary.md) shows that tRNA-binding-domain mutations can substantially improve an IFRS catalytic-pocket variant for 3BrF.

## Cross-Paper Synthesis

- Active-site mutations define much of substrate acceptance, but the apparent usefulness of that pocket depends on tRNA charging, tRNA positioning, and domain dynamics.
- Remote mutations may improve activity for a substrate by improving the enzyme-tRNA-substrate geometry rather than by directly making a larger pocket.
- The same remote mutation set can fail when the catalytic pocket or scaffold changes, so substrate-range inference must remain scaffold-specific.

## Recommendation Implication

When the target substrate is a large or halogenated aromatic amino acid, give priority to evidence from Phe-analog and IFRS-like sources. When the substrate is D-form and direct evidence is absent, label recommendations as exploration and define a control experiment.
